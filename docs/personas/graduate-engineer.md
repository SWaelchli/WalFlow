# Graduate Engineer

## Who they are
Eight months out of university with a mechanical or chemical engineering degree. Knows
Bernoulli and the Moody chart from lectures, has never sized a real system. Was told
"try WalFlow" by their supervisor. Comfortable with software, unsure of the engineering.
Arrives without an account. Represents the first-time-user lens in its purest form.

## Goals (what they want to do)
- Get a first simulation running within 5 minutes of opening the app
- Understand what each component does and which values matter
- Learn by opening the examples and the Jeppson validation benchmarks and changing them
- Avoid making a mistake that their supervisor will spot

## Constraints
- Laptop, 1440x900, sometimes on a train with a weak connection
- Does not know P&ID symbols by heart (PSV vs PCV vs check valve)
- Does not know which inputs are safe to leave at their default

## Pain triggers (what makes them close the tab)
- An empty canvas with no hint of what to do first
- Jargon with no explanation (Cv, Kv, NPSH, cracking pressure, choked flow)
- A required login before they can try anything
- Error messages that assume they know what went wrong
- Not being able to tell if a result is "good" or "bad"

## Wins (what makes them come back)
- An example opens and runs with one click and shows coloured flow on the pipes
- Hovering a field explains what it means and gives a typical value
- They change one value and can see the effect clearly

## How to audit AS this persona
- Start logged out on a fresh browser profile
- Run the 5-minute test: can a first simulation run without reading the docs?
- For every input label: would a graduate know what it means and what to type?
- Draw a deliberately wrong diagram (open end, pump against a closed valve): is the
  feedback a lesson or a dead end?
- Check the help and the Library & Examples are found without being told where they are
